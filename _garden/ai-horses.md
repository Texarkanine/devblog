## Horses

There are many kinds of horses.

* [**Draft** horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_horse) are giants that can pull heavy loads with great force.
* [**Thoroughbred** horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred) are lean and fast, bred to race and break records.
* [**Quarter** horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_horse) are fast, too, but over small distances and they're agile. Often used in ranch work.
* [**Mini** horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniature_horse) are tiny and cute, but not really ride-able.

And there are [many, many other kinds of horse](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Horse_breeds).

A mini horse will struggle to pull a plow across your fields.

A full-sized thoroughbred horse will struggle to visit children in a hospital [the way a mini horse can](https://www.stampedeoflove.org/).

And a draft horse isn't going to win the Kentucky Derby.

They're all horses, but they're all different breeds that excel (or struggle) at different things.

### Tack

In almost all cases, a horse can't *do* any of the things humans want it to, without some degree of equipment - [tack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_tack).

Want to ride a horse? Probably need to put a saddle on it.

Want it to plow a field? You'll need a [harness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_harness) to attach the horse *to* the plow so it can pull it and you can steer.

Want to win a race? You'll still need a saddle, but it's going to be a very different kind of saddle than the cowboy's using to lasso a cow.

And if you just want to walk your mini horse around town, you'll still need, at least, some kind of [halter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halter) on its head so you can do that.

There are all *kinds* of things you can strap to a horse for different tasks - from horse-drawn carriages to saddlebags, even [machine guns](https://www.reddit.com/r/wwi/comments/rrl2in/horse_with_a_machine_gun_during_ww1_german_army/).

The pattern is clear:

> Yeah, the horse itself might be "good at" or "bred for" a particular task, but it can't do it without the right supporting equipment.

### Multi-Purpose Horses

Many horses are multi-purpose animals. That draft hose that you harnessed to a plow for a day? You can probably ride him into town on a regular saddle tomorrow if you need.

The quarter-horse that herds cattle by day? It'll walk in a parade with a halter, sure thing.

Today, the mini-horse is a cute pet. Tomorrow, it's dressed as a unicorn in that same parade with the quarter-horse - horn, glitter, and all!

The horse may be "good at" or "bred for" a particular task, but with some exeptions they're not generally truly single-purpose animals. Tack them up appropriately and put them in the hands of the right equestrian, and horses can do *all kinds* of tasks for you.

Now, let's talk about AI again.

| AI | Horse |
| ---- | ---- |
| Model | Breed |
| Harness | Tack |

Claude Haiku is maybe a mini horse. Claude Opus is maybe a Draft horse.

When you launch Claude Code, you're beginning to tack up a Claude-Horse for a particular task.

If you launch Claude Code and ask Haiku-Horse to rebuild Facebook, it's not gonna happen. The mini-horse agent cannot plow the whole farm.
But if you have sufficient tokens, ol' Fable-Horse with Max effort just might.

They're both horses. You tacked them up for a task... but one horse wasn't up to it. Your fault for picking the wrong horse!

Similarly, when you launch Claude Code and Cursor and pick the *same* Claude model, you've got the same horse but now you're putting a different set of tack on it. Very similar tack, to be sure, but not identical.

Claude Opus in Cursor, versus Claude Opus in Claude Code *are* going to behave slightly differently.

Just like how if you took the same horse but tried two different saddles on it and then went for two rides, you'd get two different experiences. They'd both be horseback rides, to be sure - but not identical.

## Agents

Just like how horses are multi-purpose, Models are, too, and so can an Agent be.

You put a saddle and a halter on a quarter horse, and you can lead it around town, herd cattle, go for a run, and even go to war on it. Same horse. Same tack. Same *agent*. Equipped in a way that it can bring its natural abilities to bear on a variety of useful tasks... and then *directed to do a task*.

Claude Code and Cursor give you the tack for *coding agents* - models harnessed to be good at building software for you. [Suno](https://suno.com) harnesses a model to be good at making songs. [Midjourney](https://www.midjourney.com) equips a model to be good at making images.

Underneath *some* of those use-cases, you may even find the *same* - or at least a very similar - model. But on top of the model is a drastically different harness that equips it for a drastically different task. And then of course, *you* come along and ask it to do that task.

You're the equestrian, by the way.
